Our Story

A century in British cloth.

London and Coventry. Four generations. One trade — good cloth, honestly made.

The Linstead name has been on cloth for close to a hundred years. It started in the 1920s with a linen trade run out of Coventry — bolts of fine flax linen bought, graded and sold on to tailors and drapers across the Midlands and down to London. It was an unglamorous business built on an exacting eye: knowing, by hand and by light, which cloth would last and which would let a customer down.

That eye passed down. Through three more generations the family stayed close to the loom — in warehousing, in merchanting, in the quiet work of matching the right cloth to the right maker. We learned linen intimately: how flax is grown, retted and spun, and why a natural bast fibre behaves the way it does.

We are not a new company with an old story. We are an old cloth family with a new material.

Hemp is a bast fibre too — a close cousin of the flax our family has traded for a century. When we began looking for what came next, it wasn't a leap into something alien. It was a return to what we know: a strong, long-staple plant fibre that spins into a cloth with real backbone. We chose it because it makes a better overshirt, and because the way it grows happens to sit easily with our conscience. In that order.

Linstead & Co. is small and deliberate. We make in short runs, we tell you plainly what the cloth is and isn't, and we'd rather sell out than overstate. Drop 01 has already sold through — which is why, for now, the best thing you can do is join the list.

Why hemp — the substance, not the slogan

What the fibre actually does

Specific claims, each with the fact behind it. No vague labels.

  • Low water use

    In temperate climates hemp is largely rain-fed and needs little or no irrigation to reach maturity — far less added water than conventional cotton typically requires.

  • Grown without pesticides

    Hemp grows tall and dense, shading out weeds and resisting most pests on its own. That means the crop is typically raised without pesticide or herbicide sprays.

  • Soil-restoring

    Its deep taproot breaks up and aerates the ground, and the plant returns leaf and root matter to the soil as it grows — making hemp a genuine rotation crop that leaves the ground in better shape for the next planting.

  • Durable

    Hemp is one of the strongest natural fibres there is. Woven into canvas it resists abrasion and holds its shape, so the overshirt is built to be worn hard for years, not replaced in a season.

  • Biodegradable

    Undyed, untreated hemp fibre is a natural cellulose that breaks down at the end of its life rather than persisting as microplastic — as our Undyed Natural colourway shows, the cloth can be exactly that: fibre and nothing else.

  • Made responsibly

    Our manufacturing partner works to AZO-free, REACH-compliant standards — meaning the restricted azo dyes and chemicals covered by those regulations are not used in the cloth.

A note on our claims. We follow the UK Green Claims Code: every statement above is specific and tied to a real property of the fibre or our process. We only claim certifications we actually hold — our partner is AZO-free and REACH-compliant, and where a recognised fibre certification doesn't apply to our cloth we simply don't imply it. Where we can't yet prove a figure, we describe the mechanism honestly rather than inventing a number.
